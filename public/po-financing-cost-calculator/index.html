What purchase order financing does

PO financing pays your supplier so you can fill a confirmed customer order you couldn't otherwise afford. The funder pays the supplier directly, the goods ship to your customer, you invoice, and the invoice is usually factored or paid to the funder. Once the funder is repaid with its fees, the remaining profit is yours.

It fits resellers, distributors, importers and wholesalers with large orders from creditworthy customers, usually where the goods are finished products that ship without much work by you. It is rarely available for services or for products you manufacture yourself.

Why the timeline matters most

PO fees are charged by time, so the number that moves the result most is how long the money is out. That clock starts when the funder pays your supplier and stops when the funder is repaid. It includes production time, shipping, your customer's receiving and inspection, and your customer's payment terms. A 30-day delay at the port can add a whole fee period.

Enter a realistic day count, then try it again with 30 more days. If the order only works on the optimistic timeline, it is a risky order.

Margin is the gate

Funders generally want gross margins of 20% or more before financing, because their fees and any factoring costs come out of that margin. Run this calculator and look at two lines:

  • Profit after financing: is it still worth the work and the risk?
  • Share of profit to financing: if the funder takes more than about half of your profit, consider negotiating a deposit from your customer or better supplier terms.

Reduce what you finance

  1. Ask your customer for a deposit with the order.
  2. Ask your supplier for partial terms, such as 30% down and the balance on shipment.
  3. Shorten the timeline by shipping faster or invoicing sooner.
  4. Factor the receivable rather than waiting the full payment term, if that is cheaper.

What funders look for

Expect questions about your customer's creditworthiness, your supplier's track record, the purchase order itself, and how the goods move. A clear deal file with the PO, supplier quote, shipping plan and your margin math gets faster answers.

Frequently asked questions

How much does purchase order financing cost?

Fees often run from about 1.5% to 6% per 30 days on the amount funded. Because fees accrue over time, the total depends on how long the funder is out of pocket.

What margin do I need for PO financing?

Most funders look for gross margins of at least 20% so the order is still profitable after their fees.

Is PO financing the same as factoring?

No. PO financing pays your supplier before goods ship. Factoring advances cash against an invoice after goods are delivered. Many deals use both.

Who qualifies for PO financing?

Businesses reselling finished goods to creditworthy commercial or government customers, with a reliable supplier and a confirmed purchase order.
