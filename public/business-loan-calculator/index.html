How a business term loan payment works

A term loan is repaid in equal monthly payments. Each payment covers that month's interest first, and whatever is left reduces the balance. Early payments are mostly interest; by the final year almost the whole payment goes to principal. The schedule above shows that shift year by year.

Three inputs drive the payment: the amount borrowed, the annual interest rate, and the number of months. Stretching the term lowers the payment but raises total interest, often by more than people expect. On a $150,000 loan at 9.5%, moving from five to seven years cuts the payment by roughly $700 a month but adds several thousand dollars in interest.

What the quote leaves out

The interest rate is not the whole cost. Lenders commonly add an origination fee, a packaging or documentation fee, and sometimes a prepayment penalty. If a fee is deducted from the loan proceeds, you pay interest on money you never receive. To compare offers fairly, ask each lender for the APR, which folds required fees into one annual rate.

  • Origination fee: usually a percentage of the loan, paid at closing or taken from proceeds.
  • Prepayment penalty: a charge for paying the loan off early. Ask whether it steps down over time.
  • Variable rate: if the rate floats with prime, your payment can change. Run this calculator at a rate one or two points higher to see the risk.

Can the business afford the payment?

Lenders answer that question with the debt service coverage ratio: cash flow available for debt divided by total annual debt payments. Most want at least 1.20x to 1.25x. Multiply the monthly payment above by 12, add your other loan payments, and run the DSCR calculator before you apply. If the ratio comes up short, a longer term or a smaller amount is easier to fix now than after a decline.

Using the schedule

The interest column matters for taxes and budgeting. Business loan interest is generally deductible as a business expense, so your accountant will want the annual interest figure. The ending balance column tells you what it would take to pay the loan off or refinance at the end of any year.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a business loan payment?

Use the standard amortization formula: payment equals the loan amount times the monthly rate, divided by one minus one plus the monthly rate raised to the negative number of months. The calculator does this for you and shows the result as you type.

Is a longer term better?

A longer term lowers the monthly payment and makes the loan easier to qualify for, but you pay more interest in total. Choose the shortest term your cash flow can comfortably carry.

Does this include fees?

No. It calculates principal and interest only. Add origination and other fees separately, or ask the lender for the APR to compare offers.

Is business loan interest tax deductible?

Interest on a loan used for business purposes is generally deductible. Check with your tax preparer for your situation.
