How invoice factoring works

Factoring turns unpaid invoices into cash before your customer pays. You sell the invoice to a factor, which advances most of its value right away, usually 70% to 90%. When your customer pays the factor, it releases the remainder to you, minus its fee. That remainder is called the rebate or reserve.

Because the factor is relying on your customer's credit, factoring is often available to young or thinly capitalized businesses that can't get a bank line. The trade-off is cost.

Why the quoted rate understates the cost

A factor might quote "2.5% per 30 days." That sounds cheap next to a 10% bank loan, but the two are not on the same basis:

  • The fee is charged on the full invoice, while you only receive the advance. You pay on money you haven't received yet.
  • Fees are charged by period, rounded up. If your customer pays on day 31 of a 30-day period, you usually pay for two full periods.
  • The rate is per month, not per year. 2.5% every 30 days is about 30% a year before the other two effects.

The effective annual rate above puts the cost on the same footing as a loan APR so you can compare honestly.

Read the agreement for these terms

  • Recourse vs non-recourse: with recourse, you buy back invoices your customer doesn't pay. Non-recourse shifts some credit risk to the factor, usually only for customer insolvency, and costs more.
  • Minimum volume: some agreements charge you if you factor less than a set amount each month.
  • Extra fees: application, due diligence, wire, lockbox and termination fees all add to the true cost.
  • Notification: your customers will usually be told to pay the factor directly.

When factoring makes sense

Factoring can be worth the cost when it lets you take orders you would otherwise turn down, or when the alternative is a merchant cash advance. It is expensive as a permanent solution. If your customers routinely pay late, start by checking your days sales outstanding and tightening collections, which frees cash at no cost. Once you have steady receivables and clean books, an asset-based line of credit is usually far cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical factoring rate?

Fees commonly run from about 1% to 5% per 30 days, depending on volume, your customers' credit and how quickly they pay.

Is factoring a loan?

Usually not. You are selling the invoice rather than borrowing against it, which is why it can be easier to get than a loan. The cost still behaves like interest.

What is a factoring rebate?

The rebate, or reserve, is the part of the invoice the factor holds back until your customer pays. You receive it minus the factoring fee.

Why is the effective rate so much higher than the quoted fee?

The fee is charged per period, on the full invoice rather than the cash advanced, and periods are rounded up. Annualizing it on the advance shows the real cost.
