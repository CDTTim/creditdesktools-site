Factor rates are not interest rates

A merchant cash advance (MCA) is sold as a purchase of your future sales. The provider gives you a lump sum and collects a larger fixed amount, the payback, through daily or weekly debits from your bank account. The cost is quoted as a factor rate, such as 1.35, meaning you repay $1.35 for every $1 advanced.

A factor rate of 1.35 sounds like 35%, but that is the total cost, not an annual rate. If you repay in six months, the annualized cost is far higher than 35%, because you are paying it back quickly and the balance shrinks with every debit while the cost stays fixed.

How this calculator finds the true APR

It takes the cash you actually receive (the advance minus any fees taken out up front), the fixed payment, and the number of payments needed to reach the payback. It then solves for the interest rate that makes that stream of payments equal to the cash received, the same method used to calculate a loan's APR, and annualizes it. Triple-digit results are common for MCAs.

Paying it off early usually doesn't save money

With a loan, paying early cuts the interest. With most MCAs, the payback amount is fixed, so paying early only raises the effective rate. Some providers offer an early payoff discount; ask for it in writing before you sign.

Watch out for stacking

Taking a second advance to cover the payments on the first is called stacking. It is the most common way businesses get into serious trouble with MCAs: daily debits from two or three providers can consume most of the cash coming in. If you are considering a second advance, step back and look at your cash flow first.

Alternatives worth checking first

  • A business line of credit or term loan, even at a high rate, usually costs a fraction of an MCA.
  • Invoice factoring, if you sell to other businesses on terms.
  • Negotiating longer terms with suppliers or faster payment from customers.
  • A 13-week cash flow forecast, to see whether the shortfall is temporary or structural before you commit to daily payments.

Frequently asked questions

What is a factor rate?

The total amount you repay divided by the amount advanced. A 1.35 factor rate on $50,000 means you repay $67,500.

How do I convert a factor rate to APR?

Find the rate that makes the series of fixed payments equal the cash you received, then multiply by the number of payments per year. The calculator does this automatically.

Why is MCA APR so high?

The cost is fixed and repaid over a short period through frequent payments, so the annualized rate is much higher than the factor rate suggests.

Can I pay off a merchant cash advance early to save money?

Usually not, because the payback amount is fixed. Some providers offer an early payoff discount, so ask before you sign.
