What break-even means

Your break-even point is the level of sales where total revenue equals total costs, so you neither make nor lose money. Every sale above it adds profit; every sale below it adds to a loss. Knowing the number turns a vague goal like "sell more" into a specific monthly target.

Fixed and variable costs

The calculation depends on splitting your costs correctly:

  • Fixed costs stay the same whether you sell one unit or a thousand in a month: rent, salaried staff, insurance, software, loan payments.
  • Variable costs rise with each unit sold: materials, packaging, shipping, sales commissions, card processing fees.

Some costs are mixed. Hourly labor might be fixed up to a point and then rise with volume. Make a reasonable split and note your assumptions.

Contribution margin is the key number

Each unit's price minus its variable cost is its contribution, the amount it adds toward fixed costs and profit. Divide fixed costs by contribution and you have break-even units. A low contribution margin means a small price cut or cost increase moves break-even a long way. Try lowering the price in the calculator by 5% and watch the break-even units change.

Using it for decisions

  1. Pricing: see how many more units a discount requires to earn the same profit.
  2. Hiring or new rent: add the new fixed cost and see how many extra units cover it.
  3. Loans: include the monthly payment from the business loan calculator in fixed costs before you borrow.
  4. New products: test whether a product line can realistically reach its own break-even.

For businesses with many products

If you sell many items at different prices, use averages: average selling price and average variable cost per sale, or work in revenue terms using your overall contribution margin percentage. Break-even sales equal fixed costs divided by that percentage.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate break-even point?

Divide fixed costs by the contribution per unit, which is price minus variable cost per unit. The result is the number of units you must sell to cover costs.

What if I sell services instead of products?

Use billable hours or jobs as the unit. Price is your rate per hour or job, and variable cost is the direct cost of delivering it.

Should loan payments be in fixed costs?

For cash planning, yes. Include the full monthly payment so the break-even reflects the cash you need.

Why does a small price cut change break-even so much?

A price cut comes entirely out of contribution margin, so each unit covers less of your fixed costs and you need more units.
